# Get Moving: Run Plan & Nutrition Plan

**Strength training:** Peloton weight classes, alternating upper/lower or push/pull depending on availability (not covered here — this is your run + nutrition guide).

---

## Run Plan

Three running days per week, spaced out with rest or strength days in between (e.g., Mon/Wed/Fri or Tue/Thu/Sat). This is a 20-week progression: two single warmup weeks, a 2-week baseline, then each build-up step held for two weeks instead of one (so the ramp feels more gradual), finishing with a steady continuous-running block.

### Phase 0: Weeks 1–2 (Warmup)
- **Week 1:** All 3 days — Alternate 3 min jog / 1 min walk, for ~16 min total (4 rounds)
- **Week 2:** All 3 days — Alternate 4 min jog / 1 min walk, for ~20 min total (4 rounds)
- Purpose: build a comfortable jog/walk rhythm before extending interval length

### Phase 1: Weeks 3–4 (Confirm Baseline)
- All 3 days: Alternate 5 min jog / 1 min walk, for 24–30 min total (roughly 4 rounds)
- Purpose: lock in the pattern and make sure it feels sustainable before pushing further

### Phase 2: Weeks 5–10 (Extend the Jog Intervals)
- **Weeks 5–6:** Days 1 & 3 — 7 min jog / 1 min walk, ~28–32 min. Day 2 (easier) — 5 min jog / 1 min walk, ~24 min
- **Weeks 7–8:** Days 1 & 3 — 8 min jog / 1 min walk, ~27–36 min. Day 2 (easier) — 6 min jog / 1 min walk, ~24 min
- **Weeks 9–10:** Days 1 & 3 — 10 min jog / 1 min walk, ~30–33 min. Day 2 (easier) — 7 min jog / 1 min walk, ~24 min

### Phase 3: Weeks 11–16 (Shrink the Walk Breaks)
- **Weeks 11–12:** Days 1 & 3 — 12 min jog / 1 min walk. Day 2 (easier) — 8 min jog / 1 min walk
- **Weeks 13–14:** Days 1 & 3 — 15 min jog / 1 min walk (2 rounds), ~32 min. Day 2 (easier) — 10 min jog / 1 min walk
- **Weeks 15–16:** Day 1 — one 20 min continuous jog, then a 1 min walk, then jog out the remainder. Day 2 (easier) — 12 min jog / 1 min walk. Day 3 — repeat Day 1's structure

### Phase 4: Weeks 17–20 (Continuous Running)
- Days 1 & 3: work toward 25–35 minutes of continuous jogging without walk breaks
- Day 2 (easier/recovery run): keep this one shorter and more relaxed — 15–20 min continuous or with a walk break if needed — to avoid stacking three hard efforts back to back
- If a jog interval feels too hard on a given day, that's fine — drop back to a walk break and try again next session. Consistency matters more than a perfect progression.

### After Week 20
Repeat the structure at higher intensity (faster pace, added distance, or a third running day), or hold steady at continuous 20–30 min runs while focusing your progress on strength and nutrition instead.

### Running Notes
- Warm up with 5 minutes of brisk walking or dynamic stretches before each run
- Some muscle soreness in the first couple weeks is normal; sharp or joint pain is not — back off and adjust if that happens
- If you have any existing joint issues or heart conditions, or haven't run in a long time, it's worth checking in with a doctor before starting

## Nutrition Plan (Lose It! app)

The goal here is a moderate, sustainable calorie deficit paired with enough protein to protect muscle while you lose fat — especially since you're strength training at the same time. Lose It! is built around exactly this kind of budget-and-log approach, so the plan below maps onto its features directly.

### Setting Up Your Budget
- In Lose It!, set your **goal weight** and choose a **weekly weight-loss pace of 1–2 lbs/week** rather than the more aggressive presets — this keeps the deficit sustainable and better preserves muscle while you're strength training.
- Let the app calculate your daily calorie budget from that pace; don't override it lower than what it suggests, even if you're impatient for results. Being severely under-fueled tends to backfire (poor performance, muscle loss, rebound overeating).
- Log your weigh-ins consistently (Lose It! has a built-in weight tracker) so the app can auto-adjust your budget as you lose weight.

### Macro/Protein Tracking
- In Lose It!'s macro settings, bump your **protein target up** relative to the default — protein supports muscle retention/growth during a deficit and helps with satiety. Lose It! lets you set custom macro ranges under "Goals," so nudge protein higher and let carbs/fat fill the rest.
- Use the app's macro breakdown view (pie chart on the daily log) to check you're consistently hitting that protein target, not just the calorie number.

### Logging Habits That Work Well With the App
- Use the **barcode scanner and "Snap It" photo logging** for packaged and restaurant food — it's faster and keeps you consistent, which matters more than perfect precision.
- Save frequent meals as **custom recipes/favorites** so repeat meals (breakfast, go-to lunches) take seconds to log.
- Log food *before* eating when possible — it's easier to adjust portions in advance than to feel like you're "over budget" after the fact.
- Turn on **exercise tracking sync** if Lose It! is connected to a fitness tracker or Apple Health/Google Fit — this credits back some calories for your run days, but avoid eating back 100% of exercise calories, since Lose It!'s estimates tend to run generous.

### Practical Eating Approach
- Build meals around lean proteins, vegetables, fruits, whole grains, and healthy fats — this naturally makes it easier to stay under budget without feeling starved, and these log quickly and accurately in the app.
- Alcohol, sugary drinks, and heavily processed snacks are usually the easiest places to trim calories without much sacrifice — and they're often the items that blow a Lose It! budget the fastest, so keep an eye on them specifically.
- Use Lose It!'s **weekly summary/trends view** to look at your average intake over 7 days rather than judging any single day — day-to-day fluctuation is normal.

### Supporting the Training
- On running days, log a lighter carb-inclusive meal 1–2 hours beforehand — helps performance and is easy to plan for if you pre-log your day.
- After strength days, prioritize logging a protein source within a few hours for recovery.

### A Note on Pace
Losing 50 lbs is realistically a 6–12+ month journey at a healthy pace. Track more than the scale — how your clothes fit, your running progress, and your energy levels are all real signs of progress, especially since muscle gain from strength training can offset some scale-weight loss.
